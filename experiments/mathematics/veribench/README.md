# Environment utility for VeriBench

**Status:** Study design in progress. Baseline, supervised fine-tuning, and reinforcement-learning measurements are reported below from an external record as retrospective development evidence. The environment-utility prediction has not been tested on VeriBench.

[VeriBench](https://ehersch.github.io/veribench-blog/) evaluates whether AI coding agents can translate programs into meaningful Lean 4 artifacts with checkable specifications and theorems. This workstream studies a different question about the same target: which training environments produce the greatest held-out improvement on VeriBench at a fixed RL budget?

It extends the [controlled environment-utility study](../environment-utility/README.md) to a new candidate family and evaluation target. Candidate environments are assembled for formal code verification, measured under the starting policy, and compared through their downstream VeriBench utility curves. The goal is to predict which environments will help before paying for full RL runs.

## Measurements to date

[rl-4-code-verification](https://github.com/brando90/rl-4-code-verification) holds the experiment records for this workstream, pinned here at commit `139e8d9b47d44626035594c19d708d6b26a856cc`. Each row below links to the experiment's results file at that commit. [results/records.json](results/records.json) holds each cited figure with the SHA-256 digest, size, media type, license, and producing experiment of its source file. The page reflects this pin and changes when a new commit moves it.

**Tasks and splits.** VeriBench V1 has 896 tasks, a 614-task core and a 282-task IndustrySet, in 33 benchmark families. The external experiments label this task set `veribench@1.3` ([experiment 25 protocol](https://github.com/brando90/rl-4-code-verification/blob/139e8d9b47d44626035594c19d708d6b26a856cc/experiments/25_smooth_vs_strict_reward_grpo_qwen3_1p7b_sft_vbv1_3_d013/PROTOCOL.md#L9)) and pin its task packs at VeriBench commit `c11b99ba` and the scorer at `59059a20`. The formal-verification workstream records an unmerged Harbor v1.3 candidate at commit `c9dfd8e3b535` ([source landscape](../../cyber/formal-verification/SOURCE_LANDSCAPE.md)). This page has not compared the task manifests of the two commits, and source qualification remains pending for both. D-013 is the external repository's split of the 896 tasks into 539 train, 135 validation, and 222 test tasks. It places near-duplicate tasks and safe/vulnerable task pairs in one task cluster, and every task cluster lies in one split.

**Outcomes.** A cell is one generated response to one task. IC_tests is 1 when the Lean file typechecks and states at least one example. IC_proofs is the fraction of stated theorems that the Lean kernel accepts with axioms restricted to `propext`, `Classical.choice`, and `Quot.sound`. FSC (formal specification coverage) is a judge's rating in [0, 1] of how fully the file states the intended specification. SCSC (smooth conjunctive score for code verification) is experiment 08's aggregate score. It is the cube root of the product of the mean IC_tests, the mean IC_proofs, and the mean FSC over a model's cells, with FSC counted as 0 when IC_tests is 0. `kernel_binary` is 1 when the extracted file typechecks, has an example, states at least one theorem, every stated theorem is proved with those axioms only, and the recorded vacuous-theorem count is zero. That count is an input that each pipeline fills in, not a semantic check. Experiments 01, 05, and 25 read scorer rows without setting it, so it stays zero there, and experiment 08 fills it with a syntactic screen that flags a theorem whose conclusion is literally `True` or a self-equality. A `kernel_binary` value of 1 therefore does not establish that a theorem is non-vacuous. Experiment 19 reports strict success, which is 1 when IC_tests and IC_proofs both equal 1. Experiment 25 reports strict success as the `kernel_binary` value of each scored cell. Strict pass@1 is the mean strict success over cells. Neither `kernel_binary` nor strict success includes FSC. A group is the eight responses sampled for one task, and a task cluster is the D-013 unit defined above. A group is mixed when its responses do not all receive the same reward, and the mixed-group share is the fraction of tasks with a mixed group.

These measurements are retrospective development evidence from the benchmark authors' group. Anarres has not qualified VeriBench as a source (see [source qualification](../../cyber/formal-verification/source-qualification/README.md)), and the measurements await independent qualification. Experiments 01 and 05 evaluated all 896 tasks before D-013. Experiment 08 used D-013 train and validation for training and selection, then evaluated each selected trained checkpoint once on its 222 test tasks. Historical baseline and teacher outcomes on those tasks had already been inspected. The prospective study must name which of these partitions it holds out.

Scope and limits of each row:

- **Experiment 01.** Single call, no tools. A benchmark family is one of VeriBench's task families, which the experiment's files call a split. 31 families hold 4 probe tasks, one holds 3, and one holds 1, so each best family share rests on one mixed task.
- **Experiment 05.** Qwen3 Base only. The intervals are 95% Wilson intervals over tasks. The analysis records kernel-scored outcomes only, and FSC and SCSC were not measured.
- **Experiment 08.** The Qwen3 Base models were trained by supervised fine-tuning in bf16 with NeMo RL at revision `c751f811`, one training run per size, with training seed 20260830. Evaluation used `rl4cv.veribench.generate` with thinking on and sampler seed 91. FSC came from the `vb103_judge` agent, which requested GPT-6 Sol at high effort and Claude Opus 5.5 at max effort, with three ratings per judge. FSC is the mean of the two judges' median ratings divided by five. The judge identities are as requested: the validated bundle holds the frozen request envelope and a no-fallback receipt, and the provider-returned model and effort fields are unavailable. Selection used five checkpoints (rungs) per size, each evaluated on 135 validation tasks with eight samples per task. The selected checkpoints sit at different token fractions (8B at 1.0, 14B at 0.5), so the two sizes are unmatched. The intervals are 95% task bootstrap intervals. They resample test tasks with their eight samples and do not measure variation across training runs. Follow-up versions 2 (blocked) and 3 (incomplete at the pin) are not reported.
- **Experiment 19.** The mixed-group forecast targets one starting policy, `sft_4B_f1p0`, an experiment 08 4B checkpoint that passed no validation rung, on 135 validation tasks with eight samples each. The second endpoint uses 1,308 measured system-task outcomes from 11 systems in leave-one-system-out prediction. The 95% intervals resample the 100 D-013 task clusters that contain the 135 validation tasks and condition on the fitted models, so they exclude system sampling and fit uncertainty. The 11 folds share most of their fitting systems and are not 11 independent interventions. The experiment trains nothing.
- **Experiment 25.** The harness is `train_e25.py` started from the starting checkpoint `step_658_float16`, with vLLM sampling and the local frozen scorer, with fp16 training and evaluation, evaluation sampler seed 91, and seeds 0, 1, and 2 in each arm. No DT (differential testing). Strict pass@1 measures the local Lean/kernel criterion and does not include formal specification coverage. The evaluation leaves the vacuous-theorem count at zero, so strict success there does not establish non-vacuity. The smooth reward is a [three-factor declaration proxy](https://github.com/brando90/rl-4-code-verification/blob/139e8d9b47d44626035594c19d708d6b26a856cc/experiments/25_smooth_vs_strict_reward_grpo_qwen3_1p7b_sft_vbv1_3_d013/README.md#L8). The update-0 baseline used eight samples per task, and the update-48 evaluation used 32. The 95% cluster bootstrap intervals resample the 100 D-013 task clusters containing these 135 tasks, with the six trained models held fixed. Smooth-reward seed 2 never learned. Both seed-2 runs resumed from update 8 after a host reboot, and the resume path was not checked. Open at the pin: [a fourth smooth-reward seed is running](https://github.com/brando90/rl-4-code-verification/blob/139e8d9b47d44626035594c19d708d6b26a856cc/experiments/25_smooth_vs_strict_reward_grpo_qwen3_1p7b_sft_vbv1_3_d013/expt_v1/results/full_run_at3558/results.md#L6).
- **Experiment 09.** An infrastructure failure that says nothing about partial credit.

The VeriBench task instruction suggests `namespace` followed by the task's file stem ([baseline facts](https://github.com/brando90/rl-4-code-verification/blob/139e8d9b47d44626035594c19d708d6b26a856cc/docs/FACTS_veribench_baselines.md)). In the experiment 08 split manifest, 533 of the 896 file stems start with a digit, which a Lean identifier cannot. They are all 41 `easy_set`, all 32 `realcode_set`, and all 460 `security_set` tasks. In experiment 01, a namespace rewrite raised Qwen3-14B from 1 to 25 of 896 cells with positive IC_tests and IC_proofs. This count is a diagnostic with the task as its unit. The legacy rewrite was not guaranteed to change identifiers only, and the guarded counterfactual was not recompiled. Both cell counts come from the experiment 01 diagnostic summaries `combined_qwen3-14b_g1__0.json` and `combined_qwen3-14b_g1__1.json`, and `scripts/extract_records.py` counts the 533 stems from the manifest. The effect of the hint on the trained models in the other rows was not measured.

## Reproduce

`make study-check` runs `scripts/audit_records.py` and its negative test. The audit reads each experiment's table row together with its scope item. Within that text it requires each model-qualified score and interval, each step and update number, and each scope count to equal the figure in `results/records.json` after rounding. It also requires these statements to match figures that the extraction reads from machine-written files and scripts: the outcome definitions (allowed axioms, the conditions of `kernel_binary`, the strict-success rule of experiments 19 and 25, and the vacuity screens), the experiment 08 training, generation, and judge settings, the experiment 19 contrast and its comparator, and the experiment 25 trainer, starting checkpoint, dtype, and sampler seed. It checks the D-013 exposure statement, the namespace counts, and the release pins. It checks that each link to the external repository carries the pinned commit, that each source file records a SHA-256 digest, size, media type, license, and producing experiment, and that no source path is absolute. The negative test changes a copy of the page and of the records and requires the audit to fail. Source files write the SCSC score as SCS4CV in places, and its artifact key is `SCSC`.

The audit does not tie the following statements to a record: the namespace suggestion in the task instruction, the `veribench@1.3` label and the Harbor candidate's status, the inspection history of the test tasks, that `step_658_float16` is the experiment 08 Qwen3-1.7B checkpoint at its final step (the page relies on the experiment folder name and the step number), the smooth reward's three-factor form, fp16 evaluation, vLLM sampling and the local scorer of experiment 25, the seed-2 stall and resume history, the running fourth seed, the follow-up versions of experiment 08, and the interpretation of experiment 09.

To rebuild the records and verify the digests against the pinned commit, check out the external repository and run:

```bash
python3 scripts/extract_records.py --source PATH_TO_CHECKOUT --check
python3 scripts/audit_records.py --source PATH_TO_CHECKOUT
```

The extraction reads only the files named in `records.json`, as stored at the pinned commit. These are machine-written result files, the D-013 split manifest, and the scripts, configs, and package code that set the cited settings. The prose of the external results files is not an input. Each figure computed here carries a `derivation` and a named function in `DERIVED`. They are the exact permutation test over the six run means of experiment 25, the digit-leading stem and task-cluster counts from the split manifest, the probe tasks per benchmark family, the trained checkpoints per size, and the settings read from scripts, configs, and code.

## Relation to the formal-verification workstream

The [formal-verification workstream](../../cyber/formal-verification/README.md) qualifies Lean sources, verifiers, and runners through the `anarres formal` adapter. This study uses held-out VeriBench utility at a fixed budget as its target. A Lean candidate environment from the adapter would be assessed against that target, after source qualification.

## Why this is a new environment setting

| Component | Controlled scale study | VeriBench study |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Training tasks | Natural-language mathematical problems with answer generation | Programs that must be translated into structured Lean 4 artifacts |
| Interaction | One response followed by symbolic answer verification | Agentic tool use, Lean compilation, specification construction, and proof development |
| Reward evidence | Mathematical equivalence under one deterministic verifier | Compilation, nontrivial formal content, and coverage of the intended specification |
| Held-out target | Pass@1 on a disjoint OpenR1-Math panel | Performance on held-out VeriBench tasks |

These differences can change which experience is available, how much survives the training contract, and what counts as useful downstream improvement. The VeriBench study therefore remeasures every candidate environment under its own starting policy and tests the predictive representation again. The original four quadrants and fitted coefficients remain specific to the controlled scale study.

## Planned protocol

1. Freeze training candidates and a held-out VeriBench evaluation split by content identity.
2. Qualify Lean toolchains, task fixtures, and reward behavior before measuring learnability.
3. Measure policy-relative signal, effective coverage, generated cost, and verifier reliability without reading RL outcomes.
4. Freeze environment predictions and uncertainty rules.
5. Train the selected candidate environments under one compute-matched contract.
6. Score curve prediction, environment ordering, and allocation regret on held-out VeriBench performance.

The protocol keeps infrastructure failures separate from scientific failures and records every environment identity before claim-bearing training. Public task releases will use versioned external artifacts with dataset cards and immutable revisions.
